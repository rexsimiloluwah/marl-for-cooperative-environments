Knowledge checks appear inside the concept and research pages at the point
where a prediction or interpretation matters. They are short, specific to the
idea just developed, and provide immediate feedback for every option. Their
purpose is formative: use them to reveal which assumption produced your
answer, then revisit the nearby explanation if the feedback identifies a gap.
They do not duplicate the calculations and trade-off prompts in the chapter
worksheets.

## Using a Knowledge Check

Choose an answer before expanding any surrounding summary. After checking it,
read the feedback for both correct and incorrect options. Incorrect choices
usually represent a plausible confusion, such as treating a shared reward as
individual credit or treating centralized training as communication at
execution.

## Checks by Course Stage

- **Background:** interpret the agent-environment loop, joint actions, local
  observations, shared rewards, and Dec-POMDP components.
- **Coordinate:** predict failures of independent learning, distinguish CTDE
  mechanisms, and interpret value factorizations.
- **Communicate:** reason about useful messages, channel constraints, learned
  conventions, and protocol failures.
- **Adapt:** diagnose partner dependence and evaluate evidence about unseen
  partners.
- **Challenge Lab and final project:** transfer concepts to unfamiliar system
  descriptions and evaluation choices.
