Use this page as your **map from cooperative MARL foundations to current
research frontiers, practical application, and system design**.

## Your Learning Route

### 1. Build the Foundations

**[Background](/background/reinforcement-learning/)**

Start here if reinforcement learning or MARL is new to you.

You will build the foundation for:

- multiple agents;
- states and observations;
- joint actions and policies;
- cooperative rewards;
- partial observability;
- Dec-POMDPs.

↓

### 2. Chapter 1: Coordinate

**Question:** How should the agents' actions fit together?

Follow:

**[Introduction](/coordinate/introduction/) → Concepts → Knowledge Checks →
[Interactive Virtual Lab](/coordinate/lab/) →
[Worksheet](/coordinate/worksheet/)**

You will:

- understand the coordination problem;
- compare different training approaches;
- experiment with coordination directly in the website;
- consolidate the main ideas and trade-offs.

↓

### 3. Chapter 2: Communicate

**Question:** What information should agents share?

Follow:

**[Introduction](/communicate/introduction/) → Concepts → Knowledge Checks →
[Colab Lab](/communicate/lab/) → [Worksheet](/communicate/worksheet/)**

You will:

- understand why communication can help;
- study communication constraints and protocols;
- train agents to learn a communication protocol;
- test capacity, noise, and protocol mismatch.

↓

### 4. Chapter 3: Adapt

**Question:** What happens when the other agents change?

Follow:

**[Introduction](/adapt/introduction/) → Concepts → Knowledge Checks →
[Colab Lab](/adapt/lab/) → [Worksheet](/adapt/worksheet/)**

You will:

- distinguish familiar-partner performance from general cooperation;
- study partner diversity and agent modelling;
- evaluate agents with unseen partners;
- observe adaptation when partner behaviour changes.

↓

### 5. Explore the Frontier

## [LLMs as Cooperative Agents](/frontier/llm-agents/)

**Question:** What changes when the cooperating agents are large language
models?

Use what you learned about **Coordinate + Communicate + Adapt** to explore a
rapidly developing research direction.

You will:

- map LLM collaboration to the cooperative MARL framework;
- compare orchestrated and learned collaboration;
- connect shared rewards, credit assignment, and CTDE to multi-LLM training;
- explore recent research on learning collaboration between LLM agents;
- consider open questions around generalization, scalability, and agent
  societies.

↓

### 6. Apply Everything

## [Challenge Lab: Wireless Network Resource Allocation using MARL](/lab/overview/)

Apply:

**Coordinate + Communicate + Adapt**

You will work with wireless access points that must:

- share a limited number of channels;
- reduce interference;
- coordinate using local information;
- communicate when useful;
- remain effective under changing conditions.

↓

### 7. Create Your Own System

## [Final Project: Design a MARL-Based Disaster-Response System](/project/brief/)

Bring everything together.

You will decide:

- what the agents are;
- what they observe;
- what actions they can take;
- how they coordinate;
- what they communicate;
- how they adapt;
- how the system should be evaluated.

## Choose Your Starting Point

- **New to reinforcement learning:** start with
  [Background](/background/reinforcement-learning/).
- **Know basic RL:** begin with
  [From One Agent to Many](/background/one-agent-to-many/).
- **Know MARL foundations:** begin with
  [Chapter 1: Coordinate](/coordinate/introduction/).
- **Reviewing one topic:** jump directly to the relevant chapter.

## Within Each Chapter

Use this simple rhythm:

**Understand → Test → Experiment → Consolidate**

- **Understand:** work through the concepts and visual explanations.
- **Test:** complete the embedded knowledge checks.
- **Experiment:** use the practical lab.
- **Consolidate:** complete the worksheet.
