What changes when the critic sees more than the actor does?

A centralized critic is one way to use global information under CTDE: the critic evaluates actions during training while each actor remains a local policy for execution.

<Objectives>

- Separate the roles of the actor and the critic
- Expand the critic's inputs to the state and the joint action

## Actor and Critic Roles

Many reinforcement learning methods split the learner in two.

The <Term tone="policy">actor</Term> is the policy: the thing that chooses
actions. The <Term tone="reward">critic</Term> is a learned value or advantage
function used to improve that policy. It does not choose anything, it
*evaluates*.

<Equation
  label="Figure 1"
  wide
  tex={String.raw`\underset{\text{\scriptsize chooses}}{\tone{policy}{\pi_\theta(a \given s)}} \qquad\qquad \underset{\text{\scriptsize evaluates}}{\tone{reward}{V_\phi(s)}}`}
  terms={[
    { tex: String.raw`\pi_\theta`, is: 'the actor: the policy, with its own parameters θ', tone: 'policy' },
    { tex: String.raw`V_\phi`, is: 'the critic: a learned estimate of how good a situation is, parameters φ', tone: 'reward' },
  ]}
  caption="Two components with two jobs. Only one of them has to run at deployment."
/>

The critic exists to make the actor's updates less noisy. Instead of judging
an action by the whole messy return that followed it, the actor is nudged
according to how that action compared with what the critic expected.

And here is the observation that makes this section work: **only the actor is
needed at deployment.** The critic is training scaffolding. Once the policy is
trained, the critic is discarded.

<Callout variant="takeaway">
If the critic is thrown away before deployment, the decentralization
constraint never applies to it.

So there is no reason to keep it blind.
</Callout>

## Centralized Critic Information

That is the whole idea. Condition the critic on the centralized information
$z_t$, and leave the actor exactly as constrained as it has to be.

<Equation
  label="Figure 2"
  wide
  tex={String.raw`\tone{reward}{V_\ag\bigl(\tone{observe}{h^\ag_t},\ \tone{comm}{z_t}\bigr)} \qquad\text{or}\qquad \tone{reward}{Q_\ag\bigl(\tone{observe}{h^\ag_t},\ \tone{comm}{z_t},\ \tone{action}{\jointact}\bigr)}`}
  terms={[
    { tex: String.raw`h^\ag_t`, is: 'agent i’s own history, what it has personally seen and done', tone: 'observe' },
    { tex: String.raw`z_t`, is: 'centralized information, available because this is training', tone: 'comm' },
    { tex: String.raw`\jointact`, is: 'the joint action; an action-value critic is conditioned on what everybody did', tone: 'action' },
  ]}
  caption="Two flavours of centralized critic: one evaluates the situation, one evaluates the situation together with what the whole team did."
/>

Both forms appear in the literature. A **centralized state-value critic** is
conditioned on the agent's history plus the centralized information. A
**centralized action-value critic** is additionally conditioned on the joint
action, which, from *Coordination*, is exactly the form that can tell
combinations apart.

Meanwhile the actor is untouched:

/>

The picture is the CTDE picture with one box renamed, and that is the point. A
centralized critic is not a different arrangement. It is a specific answer to
"what consumes $z_t$?"

## Effects on the Learning Signal

The critic's job is to say how good things were. A blind critic has to do that
from one agent's partial view, which means the same observation gets the same
evaluation whether the team's other half was doing something useful or
something disastrous. Its estimates are noisy for a reason that has nothing to
do with the agent being evaluated.

Give it $z_t$ and it can see **what the team actually did**. Its estimates get
sharper, the actor's updates get less noisy, and learning gets more stable,
all without changing what the actor is allowed to know.

<Callout variant="pitfall">
Do not oversell this. A centralized critic makes the learning signal better
informed; it does not solve coordination.

The actor still chooses from local information, still cannot see its
partner's choice for the current step, and still has to arrive at a policy
that happens to fit with its partner's. A better-informed teacher does not
remove the student's blindfold, it just stops giving misleading feedback.
</Callout>

## Centralized Critics Summary

- An **actor** chooses actions; a **critic** evaluates. Only the actor is
  needed at deployment.
- A **centralized critic** is conditioned on the agent's history plus
  centralized information $z_t$; an action-value version is additionally
  conditioned on the joint action $\jointact$.
- The actor stays local: $\pol{\ag}(\act{\ag} \given h^\ag_t)$.
- The benefit is a **better-informed learning signal**, the critic can see
  what the team did, so its estimates are less confounded by partner
  behaviour.
- It does **not** solve coordination. The actor still decides alone, on
  partial information.
