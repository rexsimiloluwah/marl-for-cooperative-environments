## Mission

- Two agents must hold **both** switches down on the **same** step.
- Each agent chooses from five actions: up, down, left, right, stay.
- The team receives the reward together, or not at all.

A switch held alone earns nothing. That is the whole design.

## Try it yourself

Pick an action for each agent, then take the joint action. The environment
cannot advance on one agent's decision.

<SwitchGridPlay />

Try to reach both switches on the same step. Then try arriving early with one
agent and see what the reward does while you wait.

## Joint actions

Each agent chooses privately from its own five actions, and the environment
responds to the combination.

> ### Key Equation: Joint Action Space
>
> $$
> |\mathcal{A}| = \prod_{i=1}^{n} |\mathcal{A}_i|
> $$
>
> **Idea:** Adding agents quickly increases the number of possible joint
> decisions.

Drag the slider. Five actions per agent throughout.

<JointActionScale />

The bars are on a linear scale on purpose. A log axis would turn exponential
growth into a straight line, which is the opposite of the point.

<Callout variant="pitfall">
Notice what the count does **not** say. At six agents there are 15,625 joint
actions and no agent ever chooses among them. Each one still picks from five,
on its own information.
</Callout>

## How the agents learn

Two methods, and the difference between them is one line of arithmetic.

> ### Key Equation: Independent Q-Learning
>
> $$
> Q_i(o_i,a_i)
> \leftarrow
> Q_i(o_i,a_i)
> +
> \alpha
> \left[
> r
> +
> \gamma \max_{a_i'}Q_i(o_i',a_i')
> -
> Q_i(o_i,a_i)
> \right]
> $$
>
> **Idea:** Each agent credits itself with the whole team reward and has no
> representation of the other agent.

> ### Key Equation: Value Decomposition
>
> $$
> Q_{\mathrm{tot}} = Q_1 + Q_2
> $$
>
> **Idea:** Individual action-values are combined into a team-level value
> during learning, so one shared error is applied to both agents.

Under both methods each agent acts on its own observation: its own cell, plus
one bit saying whether the other agent is already in position.

## Train and compare

Train each method, then replay what it learned on the same grid you just
played on.

<CoordinateTraining />

What you should see at the default settings:

- Both methods reach a **100% success rate** and solve the task in **4 steps**,
  which is optimal.
- Independent learning gets there **sooner**.
- The replays are indistinguishable: both walk straight to the switches and
  hold.

<Callout variant="takeaway">
That is a real result and worth sitting with rather than explaining away.

Nothing in this task punishes miscoordination, so "walk to your switch and
stay" is a dominant strategy. Value decomposition exists to fix **credit
assignment**, and a task with no credit assignment problem gives it nothing to
fix. Its split error signal is simply slower here.

The same thing happens in the Challenge Lab, where coordination machinery
gains almost nothing and communication gains everything. Matching a mechanism
to the bottleneck you actually have is most of the skill.
</Callout>

### Make the comparison bite

The penalty control changes that. Set **Penalty for holding a switch alone**
to $-1$ or $-2$, reset training, and train both again.

Now standing on a switch while your partner is still walking costs something,
so arriving early is no longer free and a fixed plan stops being safe. Watch
what happens to the success rate and to how reliably each method converges
across the run.
