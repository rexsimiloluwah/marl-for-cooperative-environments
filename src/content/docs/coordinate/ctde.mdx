<Callout variant="predict" title="By the end of this section you can answer">
How can an agent learn from information it will not have at deployment?
</Callout>

<Term tone="policy">Centralized training with decentralized execution</Term>, or CTDE, permits global information while policies are learned but restricts each deployed agent to information it can obtain locally.

<Objectives>

- Separate what is available during training from what is available at execution
- List the centralized signals that may support learning
- Explain why CTDE is a paradigm rather than an algorithm
- State what CTDE does not guarantee

</Objectives>

## Training and Execution Phases

<CtdeSplit zLabel="z" />

During **training**, centralized information is available and is used to
shape the learning signal. During **execution**, each policy is conditioned
only on information locally available to its own agent.

<Callout variant="takeaway">
**Centralize information for learning. Decentralize decisions for execution.**
</Callout>

The important word in that sentence is *decisions*. Nothing is centralized at
execution time, not the choice, not the information, not any component. What
was centralized is already spent: it went into the gradients that produced
these policies, and then it went away.

## Centralized Training Information

Give the extra information a name so it can be used without re-listing it
every time.

<Equation
  label="Figure 1"
  tex={String.raw`\tone{comm}{z_t} \;=\; \text{whatever is available centrally at training time}`}
  terms={[
    { tex: String.raw`z_t`, is: 'centralized information at step t: available while learning, never at execution', tone: 'comm' },
  ]}
  caption="One symbol for everything training is allowed to see and execution is not."
/>

Depending on the setup, $z_t$ might contain

- the **global state** $\st$, if the simulator can provide it,
- **other agents' observations**, $\obs{j}$ for $j \neq \ag$,
- **other agents' actions**, or the whole joint action $\jointact$,
- anything else the trainer happens to have: episode metadata, partner
  identities, privileged simulator variables.

This is a genuinely free lunch in most workflows, and that is worth being
explicit about. Training usually happens in a simulator you wrote, on one
machine, with every agent's tensors in the same process. Declining to look at
them does not make your method more principled; it just discards information
you already have.

<Callout variant="pitfall">
CTDE is **not** communication, and confusing the two will cause trouble in
the Communicate chapter.

Under CTDE, $z_t$ is used by the learning algorithm and is gone at deployment.
Under communication, one agent sends information that another agent uses
*while acting*, so it has to fit in a channel, it can be lost, and it costs
something.

A quick test: if the trick still works when the deployed robots have their
radios removed, it is CTDE. If not, it is communication.
</Callout>

## Limits of CTDE

This is the correction most worth making early, because CTDE is routinely
spoken about as though it were an algorithm.

It is not. CTDE is a **training and execution paradigm**, a statement about
what information is permitted at which moment. It does not tell you:

- whether learning is **value-based or policy-based**,
- whether you use a **centralized critic**,
- whether you use **value decomposition**,
- which **algorithm** you run at all.

Those are separate design choices made *inside* CTDE, and the next four
sections are exactly those choices. A centralized critic and a value
decomposition are both ways of exploiting $z_t$; they are siblings under this
paradigm rather than alternatives to it.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Question | Answered by |
| --- | --- |
| What may training see? What may execution see? | the **paradigm**, CTDE |
| How is the centralized information actually used? | the **method**, a centralized critic, a value decomposition, … |
| Which update rule computes it? | the **algorithm**, PPO, Q-learning, … |
</div>

<Callout tone="observe" title="The constraint that survives" icon="compass">
One thing CTDE never relaxes: the deployed policies must be functions of local
information. Every method in this chapter has to produce
$\pol{\ag}(\act{\ag} \given \obs{\ag})$, or a version conditioned on that
agent's own history, no matter how much it looked at during training.

If a method needs $z_t$ at execution time, it has not solved the problem. It
has changed it.
</Callout>

## CTDE Summary

- **CTDE** allows centralized information while learning and requires local
  information while acting.
- $z_t$ names that centralized information: global state, other agents'
  observations, the joint action, or anything else training has access to.
- It is a **paradigm, not an algorithm**. It does not specify value-based
  versus policy-based learning, nor whether you use a centralized critic or a
  value decomposition. Those are choices made within it.
- It is **not communication**. $z_t$ is spent during training; a message is
  used while acting, and must fit a channel.
- The surviving constraint: deployed policies are functions of local
  information only.
