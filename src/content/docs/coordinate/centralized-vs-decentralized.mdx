Centralized and decentralized learning differ in where decisions are made and which information supports them.

<Objectives>

- Describe centralized training with centralized execution
- Describe decentralized training with decentralized execution

## Centralized Training and Execution

Suppose one learner controls everything. It sees the global state, every
agent's observation, every action taken, and the shared reward. And rather
than each agent choosing for itself, this single controller selects the whole
joint action.

<Equation
  label="Figure 1"
  tex={String.raw`\tone{action}{\jointact} = \tone{policy}{\pi_c}\bigl(\tone{observe}{\st}\bigr)`}
  terms={[
    { tex: String.raw`\pi_c`, is: 'a single central policy, the only decision-maker in the system', tone: 'policy' },
    { tex: String.raw`\jointact`, is: 'the entire joint action, chosen at once by that one policy', tone: 'action' },
    { tex: String.raw`\st`, is: 'the global state, which the controller is assumed to see', tone: 'observe' },
  ]}
  caption="One decider, everything visible, choosing for the whole team."
/>

This dissolves the coordination problem. There is no non-stationarity, because
nothing else is learning. There is no credit assignment problem, because
nothing needs crediting. There is one policy and one reward. And nothing has
to be inferred about a partner, because there are no partners.

<Callout tone="observe" title="Why this is genuinely useful" icon="compass">
Do not dismiss the centralized view. Global information is most valuable
exactly where the problem is hardest: when coordination is difficult, or when
the individual agents are severely partially observed. A central learner is
also the natural reference point, if you want to know how much your
decentralized method gave up, this is what it gave it up against.
</Callout>

So why is the rest of this chapter necessary?

**The joint action space.** With $\nag$ agents and $m$ actions each, the
controller chooses from

<Equation
  label="Figure 2"
  tex={String.raw`\bigl|\mathcal{A}\bigr| = m^{\nag}`}
  terms={[
    { tex: String.raw`|\mathcal{A}|`, is: 'the number of possible joint actions', tone: 'action' },
    { tex: String.raw`m`, is: 'the number of actions available to each agent' },
    { tex: String.raw`\nag`, is: 'the number of agents' },
  ]}
  caption="A centralized controller chooses from a joint-action space that grows exponentially."
/>

Ten agents with five actions each is nearly ten million options at every
single step. The textbook identifies this exponential growth in the joint
action space as a central limitation of centralized learning, and it is not a
limitation you engineer around. It is the size of the thing being learned.

**Physical distribution.** Even where the space is small enough, centralized
control may simply be unavailable. A central controller needs every
observation, in time, every step, and it needs to broadcast every action back.
Robots on a warehouse floor, vehicles from different manufacturers, and agents
owned by different organizations may not support that assumption before the
algorithm is even considered.

## Decentralized Training and Execution

The opposite corner. Each agent learns from its own experience and acts on its
own observation, with no shared information at any point. This is the fully
decentralized form of independent learning.

<Equation
  label="Figure 3"
  tex={String.raw`\tone{action}{\act{\ag}} \sim \tone{policy}{\pol{\ag}}\bigl(\act{\ag} \given \tone{observe}{\obs{\ag}}\bigr) \quad \text{for each } \ag \in \mathcal{N}`}
  terms={[
    { tex: String.raw`\pol{\ag}`, is: 'one policy per agent, trained on that agent’s own experience only', tone: 'policy' },
    { tex: String.raw`\obs{\ag}`, is: 'its own observation, the only input available', tone: 'observe' },
  ]}
  caption="Nothing shared, at training or at execution. Scalable, deployable, and blind to partners."
/>

The practical advantages of independent learning still apply: it scales, it deploys, it
needs no infrastructure. So does every cost: non-stationarity, and no use of
information about the other agents even when that information exists.

## Training and Execution as Separate Axes

Laying the modes out side by side makes the opening visible.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Mode | Information while learning | Information while acting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Centralized training **and** execution | everything | everything |
| Decentralized training **and** execution | local only | local only |
| **Centralized training, decentralized execution** | everything | **local only** |
</div>

The third row is the one that matters, and notice that it is not a compromise
between the first two. It takes the *advantage* of the first, full
information where the learning happens, and the *constraint* of the second,
where the constraint is unavoidable anyway.

<Callout variant="takeaway">
The insight is that these are different moments with different requirements.

At **execution** time, local information is a hard constraint: it is set by
physics, by network topology, by what sensors exist. You cannot negotiate with
it.

At **training** time it is not a constraint at all. Training usually happens
in a simulator you control, on one machine, with every agent's observation and
action sitting in memory. Refusing to look at them buys nothing.

> **Can we use global information to make training easier, without requiring
> global information when the agents actually act?**
</Callout>

That question is the next section.

## Centralized and Decentralized Learning Summary

- Two independent questions: what information is available during
  **training**, and what is available during **execution**.
- **Centralized training and execution** removes coordination, credit
  assignment and non-stationarity in one stroke, and is limited by an
  exponential joint action space $m^\nag$ plus the practical impossibility of
  central control in distributed systems.
- **Decentralized training and execution**, independent learning, scales and
  deploys, but gives up all information about partners.
- Local information is a **hard constraint at execution** and usually **no
  constraint at all during training**. That asymmetry is the opening the rest
  of the chapter exploits.
